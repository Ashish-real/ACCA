2.1
What is Accounting? — The Purpose & Power

Accounting is the process of identifying, recording, measuring, classifying, summarising, interpreting, and communicating financial information. It converts the messy reality of business transactions into clean, structured data that decision-makers can use.

📝
Record Keeping
  • Track every rupee that comes in and goes out
  • Maintain a permanent, organised record
  • Legal requirement for all businesses
Bookkeeping
📊
Decision Making
  • Give managers accurate financial information
  • Help decide: invest, cut costs, expand?
  • Budget planning and forecasting
Management Accounting
🌍
External Reporting
  • Report to shareholders, investors, banks
  • Comply with tax authorities (Income Tax, GST)
  • Meet regulatory requirements (SEBI, RBI, MCA)

The Accounting Equation
Foundation of Everything
ASSETS = LIABILITIES + EQUITY
The Fundamental Accounting Equation — always in balance, forever.
🏦
Assets
  • Resources owned by the business
  • Cash, bank, inventory, equipment, land
  • Intangibles: patents, goodwill, brand
What you OWN
💳
Liabilities
  • Obligations owed by the business
  • Bank loans, creditors, tax payable
  • Long-term debt, debentures, bonds
What you OWE
👑
Equity (Capital)
  • Owner's claim on business assets
  • Share capital + retained earnings
  • Increases with profit, decreases with loss
What OWNERS are owed

2.4
Debit & Credit — The Golden Rules
Every Transaction = At least one DEBIT + at least one CREDIT
Total Debits ALWAYS = Total Credits
Double-Entry Bookkeeping — invented by Luca Pacioli in 1494. ACCA still uses it today.
Account Type To INCREASE To DECREASE
Asset DEBIT ↑ Credit ↓
Liability CREDIT ↑ Debit ↓
Equity / Capital CREDIT ↑ Debit ↓
Income / Revenue CREDIT ↑ Debit ↓
Expense DEBIT ↑ Credit ↓

2.6
The Ledger & Trial Balance
📒
The General Ledger
  • Master book of all accounts
  • Each account has its own T-account page
  • Left side = Debits | Right side = Credits
  • Modern firms: SAP, Tally, Xero, QuickBooks
Book of Secondary Entry
⚖️
Trial Balance
  • List of all ledger account balances
  • Two columns: Total Dr | Total Cr
  • If balanced → arithmetic is correct
  • Prepared at end of accounting period
Arithmetic Check
⚠️
Errors TB Won't Catch
  • Error of omission (transaction not recorded)
  • Error of commission (wrong account, same type)
  • Error of principle (wrong account type)
  • Compensating errors (two cancel out)
ACCA FA Exam Topic
2.7
The Accounting Cycle — End to End
Step 1
🧾 Identify & Analyse Transactions
Every business event with financial impact is identified. Source documents: invoices, receipts, bank statements.
Step 2
📝 Record in Journal
Each transaction is recorded as a journal entry with debit and credit accounts — the "book of original entry."
Step 3
📒 Post to Ledger
Journal entries transferred to individual ledger accounts. Each account now shows its running balance.
Step 4
⚖️ Prepare Trial Balance
Extract all ledger balances. Verify total debits = total credits. Find and correct any errors.
Step 5
🔧 Adjusting Entries
Record accruals, prepayments, depreciation, and provisions to match revenues and expenses to the correct period.
Step 6
📊 Prepare Financial Statements
Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity — all from the adjusted trial balance.
Step 7
🔄 Closing Entries
Close all temporary accounts (income, expenses) to retained earnings. Income and expense accounts reset to zero for next period.

2.9
Cash vs Accrual Accounting
💵
Cash Accounting
  • Record income only when cash is received
  • Record expense only when cash is paid
  • Simple — used by small businesses
  • Not allowed under IFRS for large entities
Simple · Incomplete
📅
Accrual Accounting
  • Record income when earned (not when received)
  • Record expense when incurred (not when paid)
  • Required by IFRS and Companies Act
  • Used by all listed companies and MNCs
IFRS · Required · Accurate
🔄
Accruals & Prepayments
  • Accrued expense: incurred, not yet paid
  • Prepaid expense: paid, not yet incurred
  • Accrued income: earned, not yet received
  • Deferred income: received, not yet earned
ACCA FA Core Topic

Common Mistakes, Flashcards & Interview Q&A

Part A — Most Common Mistakes (Don't Repeat These)

❌ MISTAKE 1
Confusing Debit/Credit With "Increase/Decrease"
Debit and Credit don't inherently mean increase or decrease — their effect depends on the account type. A debit increases assets and expenses but decreases liabilities, equity, and income. Memorising "debit = increase" universally leads to systematic errors.
❌ MISTAKE 2
Forgetting Every Transaction Needs Equal Debits and Credits
Students sometimes post only one side of an entry, or unequal amounts. Double-entry bookkeeping requires Total Debits = Total Credits for every single transaction, without exception — this is what keeps the accounting equation balanced.
❌ MISTAKE 3
Mixing Up Cash Basis and Accrual Basis
Under accrual accounting (required by IFRS), revenue and expenses are recorded when earned/incurred, not when cash changes hands. A common exam error is recording revenue only when payment is received, which is cash-basis thinking — not IFRS compliant.
❌ MISTAKE 4
Not Distinguishing Capital vs Revenue Expenditure
Buying a machine (capital expenditure) should be capitalised on the Balance Sheet and depreciated over time — not expensed immediately like a revenue expense (e.g. repairs, salaries). Treating capital items as revenue expenses distorts both profit and asset values.
❌ MISTAKE 5
Skipping the Trial Balance Check
Before preparing financial statements, always extract a Trial Balance to confirm Total Debits = Total Credits. Skipping this step means errors flow directly into the final statements, making them far harder to trace and fix.
❌ MISTAKE 6
Applying the Matching Concept Incorrectly
The Matching Concept requires expenses to be recognised in the same period as the revenue they help generate — not simply in the period cash was paid. Misapplying this leads to overstated or understated profit in a given period.
❌ MISTAKE 7
Confusing Prepaid Expenses with Accrued Expenses
A prepaid expense is cash paid in advance for a future benefit (an asset). An accrued expense is a cost already incurred but not yet paid (a liability). Mixing these up is one of the most frequent errors in ACCA FA adjustment questions.

Part D — Interview Questions & Model Answers

Question What They Are Testing Model Answer
"Explain double-entry bookkeeping in simple terms." Can you explain a technical concept simply? Every financial transaction affects at least two accounts — one debited and one credited by equal amounts. For example, buying equipment with cash debits Equipment (asset increases) and credits Cash (asset decreases) — the equation stays balanced because total value hasn't changed, just its form.
"What's the difference between cash and accrual accounting?" Do you understand the basis IFRS actually requires? Cash accounting records transactions only when cash is received or paid. Accrual accounting — required under IFRS — records revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of when cash moves. This gives a more accurate picture of performance for a given period, which is why virtually all companies of scale use accrual accounting.
"Why does the trial balance matter if it doesn't guarantee no errors?" Understanding limitations of accounting controls The trial balance confirms total debits equal total credits, catching errors like one-sided entries or arithmetic mistakes. However, it won't catch errors of principle (wrong account used correctly on both sides), complete omission of a transaction, or compensating errors — so it's a necessary but not sufficient check.
