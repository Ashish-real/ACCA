In Chapter 3, you learned how to prepare financial statements. But who decides the rules for how those statements must be prepared? The answer is IFRS — International Financial Reporting Standards. These are the global accounting rules used by over 140 countries and every major MNC in the world. ACCA is built on IFRS — understanding it is non-negotiable.

"IFRS is the common language of global finance. If you speak IFRS, you can work anywhere in the world."
4.1
What is IFRS — and Why It Exists

Before IFRS, every country had its own accounting rules. A company's profit in Germany looked different from the same company's profit in Japan — even if the underlying business was identical. This made global investing, lending, and comparison impossible. IFRS was created to solve this problem.

🌍
What IFRS Is
  • International Financial Reporting Standards
  • Set by the IASB (International Accounting Standards Board)
  • Based in London, UK
  • Used in 140+ countries including India (as Ind AS)
  • Mandatory for listed companies in most countries
Global Standard
🎯
Why IFRS Was Created
  • Allow global comparison of financial statements
  • Increase transparency and reduce fraud
  • Make cross-border investment easier
  • Give investors one common language worldwide
  • Reduce cost of preparing multiple sets of accounts
Standardisation
🏛️
Who Sets IFRS
  • IASB — writes and issues the standards
  • IFRS Foundation — oversees IASB
  • IFRS Advisory Council — stakeholder input
  • IFRIC — interprets standards in practice
  • Each country's regulator adopts and enforces locally
IASB · London

Brief History of IFRS

1973
IASC Founded
International Accounting Standards Committee (IASC) formed — the predecessor to IASB. First international standards called IAS (International Accounting Standards) begin to be issued.
2001
IASB Replaces IASC
IASB takes over. New standards issued from here are called IFRS. Old IAS standards are retained and still in use today (e.g. IAS 1, IAS 16, IAS 38).
2005
EU Adopts IFRS
All listed companies in the European Union must use IFRS. Major turning point — suddenly 7,000+ companies switch to IFRS simultaneously.
2011
India Announces Ind AS
India announces IFRS-converged standards called Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS). Phased adoption begins from 2016 for large companies.
2024+
IFRS S1 & S2 — Sustainability
IASB launches sustainability reporting standards (IFRS S1, S2) — the future of corporate reporting. ESG disclosures becoming mandatory globally.
4.2
The Conceptual Framework
The Constitution of Accounting

Before any specific standard, IFRS has a Conceptual Framework — think of it as the constitution of accounting. It sets out the objectives, concepts, and principles that guide all IFRS standards. When there is no specific standard for a situation, accountants fall back on the Framework.

The Conceptual Framework — Key Elements
Objective
To provide financial information that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders, and creditors in making decisions about providing resources to an entity.
Reporting Entity
Any entity (company, group, subsidiary) that is required or chooses to prepare financial statements in accordance with IFRS.
Elements
Five elements of financial statements: Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Income (Revenue + Gains), Expenses (including Losses).
Recognition
An element is recognised in financial statements when it meets the definition AND it is probable that future economic benefits will flow AND it can be measured reliably.
Measurement
Historical cost, fair value, current cost, or present value — the Framework identifies all bases of measurement used across IFRS standards.
Capital Maintenance
Profit is earned only after the capital invested has been maintained. Financial capital maintenance vs physical capital maintenance concepts.
4.3
Qualitative Characteristics
What Makes Good Financial Information

The Framework sets out qualitative characteristics that make financial information useful. These are tested heavily in ACCA FA and FR papers. Know them by heart.

Fundamental Qualitative Characteristics

✅
Relevance
  • Information that makes a difference to decisions
  • Has predictive value (helps forecast future)
  • Has confirmatory value (confirms past decisions)
  • Materiality is part of relevance
  • Immaterial items need not be disclosed
Fundamental
🔒
Faithful Representation
  • Information must be complete — no omissions
  • Must be neutral — no bias
  • Must be free from error — no mistakes
  • Replaces old concept of "reliability"
  • Substance over form — economic reality wins
Fundamental

Enhancing Qualitative Characteristics

🔄
Comparability
  • Same methods used period to period
  • Allows comparison across companies
  • Changes in policy disclosed in notes
Enhancing
✔️
Verifiability
  • Independent observers can reach same conclusion
  • Auditors verify financial information
  • Direct or indirect verification
Enhancing
⏱️
Timeliness
  • Available in time to influence decisions
  • Old information loses relevance
  • Annual vs quarterly reporting debate
Enhancing
💡
Understandability
  • Clear and concise presentation
  • Assumes a reasonably knowledgeable user
  • Complex information still disclosed
Enhancing
⚡ Cost Constraint
The Framework recognises one overriding constraint: the cost of providing information must be justified by the benefits. If it costs more to report something than the value it provides to users, it need not be reported. Examiners test this in application questions.

🇮🇳 India's Position — Ind AS
India does not use IFRS directly. Instead, India uses Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) — which are substantially converged with IFRS but with some carve-outs (modifications). For ACCA students: ACCA teaches pure IFRS. When you work in India, you'll apply Ind AS — but since Ind AS is ~95% the same as IFRS, your knowledge transfers directly.

The ACCA Essential List

There are 17 active IFRS standards and 28 active IAS standards. You don't need to memorise all of them for now — but the ones below are essential for ACCA and your MNC career. Each will be covered in full detail in later chapters.

IAS 1

Rules for how the 4 financial statements must be structured and what must be disclosed. The most foundational standard.

Rules for preparing the cash flow statement. Direct and indirect methods. Operating, investing, financing classification.

When and how to write down the value of assets. Recoverable amount = higher of fair value less costs and value in use.

4.7
Fair Value vs Historical Cost
The Great Accounting Debate

One of the biggest debates in accounting — and a key exam topic — is whether assets should be recorded at their original cost (historical cost) or at their current market value (fair value). IFRS allows both, depending on the standard.

FeatureHistorical CostFair Value
DefinitionOriginal purchase price — what you paidPrice you would receive if you sold today
ReliabilityVery reliable — based on actual transactionLess reliable — based on market estimates
RelevanceLess relevant — old price may not reflect realityMore relevant — reflects current economic value
VerifiabilityEasy — invoice, receipt, contractHarder — requires valuation, market data
Used ForPPE (cost model), inventory, most assetsInvestment property, financial instruments, biological assets
IFRS StandardIAS 16 (cost model), IAS 2IFRS 13 (Fair Value Measurement), IAS 40
P&L ImpactStable — depreciation is predictableVolatile — gains/losses when values change
📌 IFRS 13 — Fair Value Measurement
IFRS 13 defines fair value as "the price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date." It also introduces the Fair Value Hierarchy — Level 1 (quoted prices), Level 2 (observable inputs), Level 3 (unobservable inputs). This hierarchy determines how reliable a fair value measurement is.

🇮🇳 Who Must Use Ind AS in India?

Phase 1 (2016–17): Companies with net worth ≥ ₹500 crore OR listed on stock exchanges
Phase 2 (2017–18): Companies with net worth ≥ ₹250 crore
Banks & Insurance: Separate phased timeline set by RBI/IRDAI

Bottom line: If you work at any Big 4 or large MNC in India, you will use Ind AS — which means your ACCA IFRS knowledge applies directly.

Common Mistakes, Flashcards & Interview Q&A

Part A — Common Mistakes (Don't Repeat These)

❌ MISTAKE 1
Confusing IAS and IFRS Numbering
IAS standards (issued by the old IASC, pre-2001) and IFRS standards (issued by the IASB, post-2001) coexist — both are current and binding. Students often assume "IFRS" replaces all "IAS" standards, but many IAS standards (like IAS 1, IAS 2, IAS 16) remain fully in force today.
❌ MISTAKE 2
Treating the Conceptual Framework as a Standard
The Conceptual Framework is NOT an IFRS standard itself — it cannot override a specific standard's requirements. It's a foundational reference used when no standard addresses a particular issue, or to help interpret existing standards. Citing "the Framework" over a specific IAS/IFRS is a common exam error.
❌ MISTAKE 3
Assuming Ind AS = IFRS Exactly
Ind AS is IFRS-converged but not identical — there are specific carve-outs and modifications (e.g. certain financial instrument classifications, some presentation differences). Assuming 100% equivalence can mislead in a India-specific context.
❌ MISTAKE 4
Mixing Up Relevance and Faithful Representation
Relevance is about whether information CAN influence a decision (predictive/confirmatory value, materiality). Faithful representation is about whether information is complete, neutral, and free from error. Students often merge these two fundamental qualitative characteristics into one vague idea.
❌ MISTAKE 5
Believing Fair Value is Always "Better" Than Historical Cost
Fair value is more relevant (reflects current market conditions) but less reliable (subjective, can fluctuate). Historical cost is more reliable (objective, verifiable) but less relevant over time. IFRS uses a mix of both depending on the standard — there's no universal "better" measurement basis.
❌ MISTAKE 6
Forgetting US GAAP is Rules-Based, IFRS is Principles-Based
This is a frequently tested distinction — US GAAP tends to provide detailed, prescriptive rules for specific scenarios, while IFRS provides broader principles requiring professional judgment. Confusing which system is which is a common exam trap.
