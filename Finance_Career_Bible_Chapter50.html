MA (Management Accounting) — Applied Knowledge

Cost behaviour, break-even, and variance analysis — the calculation core of MA.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
Which of these costs is classified as variable?A) Factory rent
B) Straight-line depreciation
C) Direct material ✅
D) Factory manager's salary		C — Direct material cost moves in direct proportion to production volume; the other three are typically fixed.
A company has selling price ₹80/unit, variable cost ₹50/unit, and fixed costs of ₹1,50,000. What is the break-even point in units?A) 3,000 units
B) 5,000 units ✅
C) 1,875 units
D) 2,500 units		B — Contribution/unit = ₹80−₹50 = ₹30. BEP = ₹1,50,000 ÷ ₹30 = 5,000 units.
Material Price Variance is calculated as:A) (Standard Qty − Actual Qty) × Standard Price
B) (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Actual Quantity ✅
C) (Actual Price − Standard Price) × Standard Quantity
D) (Standard Price − Actual Price) × Standard Quantity		B — Price variance isolates the price effect — it's always calculated on the ACTUAL quantity purchased/used.
If Margin of Safety increases, this means:A) The business is closer to break-even and riskier
B) The business has more cushion above break-even — lower risk ✅
C) Fixed costs have increased
D) Contribution per unit has decreased		B — A larger Margin of Safety (Actual Sales − Break-Even Sales) means more room before the business starts making a loss.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Break-Even, Margin of Safety & Profit

Question: Product X sells for ₹500/unit. Variable cost is ₹300/unit. Annual fixed costs are ₹8,00,000. Budgeted sales are 5,000 units. Calculate (a) Break-even point in units, (b) Margin of Safety in units and as a %, (c) Budgeted profit.

Step 1 — Contribution per unit: ₹500 − ₹300 = ₹200
Step 2 — Break-Even Point: Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution = ₹8,00,000 ÷ ₹200 = 4,000 units
Step 3 — Margin of Safety: 5,000 − 4,000 = 1,000 units → 1,000 ÷ 5,000 × 100 = 20%
Step 4 — Budgeted Profit: Total Contribution (5,000 × ₹200 = ₹10,00,000) − Fixed Costs (₹8,00,000) = ₹2,00,000

Answer: BEP = 4,000 units | Margin of Safety = 1,000 units (20%) | Budgeted Profit = ₹2,00,000

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
Purchasing a delivery van on credit affects the accounting equation by:A) Increasing Assets and Increasing Equity
B) Increasing Assets and Increasing Liabilities ✅
C) Decreasing Assets and Increasing Liabilities
D) No effect — it's an off-balance-sheet transaction		B — The van (asset) increases, and since it's on credit, a payable (liability) increases by the same amount — the equation stays balanced.
A machine costs ₹1,20,000, has an estimated residual value of ₹12,000, and a useful life of 6 years. Annual straight-line depreciation is:A) ₹20,000
B) ₹18,000 ✅
C) ₹22,000
D) ₹16,000		B — (₹1,20,000 − ₹12,000) ÷ 6 = ₹18,000 per year.
Under IAS 2, inventory is valued at:A) Historical cost only
B) Net Realisable Value only
C) Lower of Cost and Net Realisable Value ✅
D) Fair value through profit or loss		C — IAS 2 requires the LOWER of cost and NRV — this prevents overstating inventory when its value has fallen.
In a trial balance, if total debits do not equal total credits, this most likely indicates:A) A compensating error
B) A one-sided entry or arithmetic error ✅
C) An error of principle
D) An error of omission		B — Errors of principle, omission, and compensating errors do NOT cause a trial balance imbalance since both sides are still equal (just wrong) — only a one-sided or arithmetic error breaks the balance.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Statement of Profit or Loss — Extract

Question: From the following figures, prepare a Statement of Profit or Loss extract down to Operating Profit: Revenue ₹5,00,000; Cost of Goods Sold ₹3,00,000; Operating Expenses ₹80,000; Depreciation ₹20,000.

Step 1 — Gross Profit: Revenue (₹5,00,000) − COGS (₹3,00,000) = ₹2,00,000
Step 2 — Deduct Operating Expenses and Depreciation: ₹2,00,000 − ₹80,000 − ₹20,000 = ₹1,00,000

Answer: Gross Profit = ₹2,00,000 | Operating Profit (EBIT) = ₹1,00,000

PM (Performance Management) — Applied Skills

Learning curves, throughput accounting, and advanced variance analysis.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
A Throughput Accounting Ratio (TPAR) greater than 1 indicates:A) The product destroys value and should be dropped
B) The product is profitable to produce at the bottleneck ✅
C) Fixed costs exceed variable costs
D) The learning curve has ended		B — TPAR > 1 means throughput per bottleneck minute exceeds factory cost per bottleneck minute — the product generates value faster than it costs.
The learning curve effect assumes that as cumulative output doubles, the cumulative average time per unit:A) Increases by a fixed percentage
B) Falls by a constant percentage ✅
C) Remains unchanged
D) Falls to zero		B — This is the defining property of a learning curve — e.g. an 80% curve means cumulative average time falls to 80% of its previous level each time output doubles.
In mix and yield variance analysis, the Mix Variance isolates the effect of:A) Using a different total quantity of input than budgeted
B) Using a different PROPORTION of materials than the standard mix ✅
C) A change in the selling price
D) A change in labour efficiency		B — Mix variance measures the cost impact of blending materials in a different ratio than standard — separate from the total quantity (yield) effect.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Learning Curve — Incremental Time Calculation

Question: The first unit of a new product takes 100 hours to produce, and an 80% learning curve applies. Calculate the total time for the first 8 units, and the time taken for the 8th unit alone.

Step 1 — b value: b = log(0.8) ÷ log(2) = −0.3219
Step 2 — Cumulative average time for 8 units: Y = 100 × 8^(−0.3219) = 100 × 0.512 = 51.2 hours. Total time for 8 units = 51.2 × 8 = 409.6 hours
Step 3 — Cumulative average time for 7 units: Y = 100 × 7^(−0.3219) = 53.45 hours. Total time for 7 units = 53.45 × 7 = 374.14 hours
Step 4 — Time for the 8th unit: Total(8) − Total(7) = 409.6 − 374.14 = 35.46 hours

Answer: Total time for first 8 units = 409.6 hours | Time for the 8th unit alone ≈ 35.46 hours

The Health & Education Cess applied on corporate tax (plus surcharge) in India is:A) 2%

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Corporate Tax Liability — Section 115BAA

Question: A domestic company has taxable income of ₹50,00,000 and opts for the concessional regime under Section 115BAA (22% base rate, 10% surcharge, 4% cess). Calculate the total tax liability and the effective tax rate.

Step 1 — Base Tax: 22% × ₹50,00,000 = ₹11,00,000
Step 2 — Surcharge: 10% × ₹11,00,000 = ₹1,10,000
Step 3 — Cess: 4% × (₹11,00,000 + ₹1,10,000) = 4% × ₹12,10,000 = ₹48,400
Step 4 — Total Tax Liability: ₹11,00,000 + ₹1,10,000 + ₹48,400 = ₹12,58,400

Answer: Total Tax Liability = ₹12,58,400 | Effective Tax Rate = ₹12,58,400 ÷ ₹50,00,000 = 25.17%

C) As a contingent liability only

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Goodwill on Acquisition

Question: A parent acquires 80% of a subsidiary for cash consideration of ₹40,00,000. The fair value of the 20% non-controlling interest at acquisition is ₹9,00,000. The fair value of the subsidiary's identifiable net assets at acquisition is ₹45,00,000. Calculate goodwill on acquisition.

Formula: Goodwill = Consideration Paid + Fair Value of NCI − Fair Value of Identifiable Net Assets
Calculation: ₹40,00,000 + ₹9,00,000 − ₹45,00,000

AA (Audit & Assurance) — Applied Skills

Audit risk, materiality, and internal control.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
Per the Audit Risk Model, Audit Risk equals:A) Inherent Risk + Control Risk + Detection Risk
B) Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk ✅
C) Inherent Risk − Control Risk
D) Detection Risk ÷ Control Risk		B — The three risk components MULTIPLY together (not add) to give overall Audit Risk.
Segregation of duties is a control designed primarily to prevent:A) Slow processing of transactions
B) One individual having the ability to both commit and conceal an error or fraud ✅
C) High audit fees
D) Inventory obsolescence		B — Splitting authorisation, custody, and recording functions across different people makes it much harder for any single person to commit AND hide fraud.
Which of the following is the strongest form of audit evidence?A) Verbal representation from management
B) Auditor's own physical inspection / third-party confirmation ✅
C) Client-prepared internal memo
D) Prior year's unaudited figures		B — Evidence obtained directly by the auditor or from independent third parties is generally more reliable than evidence from the client itself.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Audit Risk Model — Solving for Detection Risk

Question: An auditor wants to achieve an overall audit risk of 5%. Inherent Risk is assessed at 80% and Control Risk at 50%. What level of Detection Risk should the auditor design substantive procedures to achieve?

Formula: AR = IR × CR × DR → DR = AR ÷ (IR × CR)
Calculation: DR = 0.05 ÷ (0.80 × 0.50) = 0.05 ÷ 0.40

Answer: Detection Risk = 12.5% — the lower this figure, the MORE extensive substantive testing the auditor must perform.

Investment appraisal, cost of capital, and working capital management.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
In the CAPM formula Ke = Rf + β(Rm − Rf), the term (Rm − Rf) represents:A) The risk-free rate
B) The Equity Risk Premium ✅
C) The company's Beta
D) The cost of debt		B — (Rm − Rf) is the extra return investors demand for taking on equity market risk over a risk-free investment.
Which investment appraisal method is theoretically superior because it directly measures value created in absolute currency terms?A) Payback Period
B) Accounting Rate of Return
C) Net Present Value ✅
D) None — they're all equivalent		C — NPV directly measures the increase in shareholder wealth in ₹ terms, discounted for time value of money — the theoretical gold standard.
A key limitation of the Payback Period method is that it:A) Is too complex to calculate
B) Ignores the time value of money and cash flows after the payback date ✅
C) Cannot be used for capital-intensive projects
D) Always gives the same answer as NPV		B — Payback is simple and liquidity-focused but ignores discounting and anything that happens after the cutoff point — a real weakness for long-term decisions.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Net Present Value (NPV)

Question: A project requires an initial investment of ₹10,00,000 and is expected to generate cash inflows of ₹3,00,000 per year for 5 years. The company's cost of capital is 10% (5-year annuity factor at 10% = 3.7908). Calculate the NPV and recommend whether to accept the project.

Step 1 — PV of Inflows: ₹3,00,000 × 3.7908 = ₹11,37,240
Step 2 — NPV: PV of Inflows − Initial Investment = ₹11,37,240 − ₹10,00,000 = ₹1,37,240
Decision Rule: Accept if NPV > 0

Answer: NPV = ₹1,37,240 (positive) → Accept the project, as it is expected to increase shareholder wealth.

Under IAS 21, monetary items on the Statement of Financial Position are translated at:A) The historical rate at the transaction date
B) The closing (year-end) rate ✅

AFM (Advanced Financial Management) — Strategic Professional

Adjusted Present Value and valuation multiples.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
The Adjusted Present Value (APV) method is preferred over a standard WACC-based NPV when:A) The project is very small
B) The capital structure changes significantly over the project's life ✅
C) The company has no debt at all
D) Tax rates are zero		B — APV separates the base-case (all-equity) NPV from financing side-effects, making it more robust when gearing changes materially during the project.
EV/EBITDA is generally preferred over P/E for cross-company comparison because it is:A) Always a larger number
B) Capital-structure neutral — unaffected by differing debt levels between companies ✅
C) Based only on historical cost accounting

📊 Adjusted Present Value (APV)

Question: A project has a base-case (all-equity) NPV of ₹5,00,000. Financing the project with debt creates a PV of tax shield benefit of ₹60,000, but debt issue costs of ₹20,000 are also incurred. Calculate the APV.

Formula: APV = Base-Case NPV + PV of Financing Side-Effects (Tax Shield) − Issue Costs
Calculation: ₹5,00,000 + ₹60,000 − ₹20,000

Divisional performance measures and transfer pricing.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
Residual Income (RI), unlike ROI, has the advantage of:A) Being expressed as a simple percentage, easy to compare across divisions of different sizes
B) Encouraging managers to accept any project that exceeds the cost of capital, avoiding ROI's dilution problem ✅
C) Requiring no knowledge of the cost of capital
D) Being unaffected by asset valuation policy		B — ROI can cause a manager to REJECT a project that's good for the company (because it dilutes their average ROI) — RI avoids this by using an absolute ₹ measure against a required return.
The best transfer pricing method to use when a genuine external market for the product exists is:A) Full Cost basis
B) Cost-Plus basis
C) Market Price basis ✅
D) Negotiated price with no reference point		C — Market price gives an objective, externally-verifiable transfer price and avoids internal disputes over cost allocation or margin fairness.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 ROI and Residual Income

Question: A division has annual profit of ₹12,00,000 and capital employed of ₹60,00,000. The company's required rate of return (cost of capital) is 12%. Calculate the division's ROI and Residual Income.

ROI: Divisional Profit ÷ Capital Employed × 100 = ₹12,00,000 ÷ ₹60,00,000 × 100 = 20%
Residual Income: Profit − (Capital Employed × Required Return) = ₹12,00,000 − (₹60,00,000 × 12%) = ₹12,00,000 − ₹7,20,000

Answer: ROI = 20% | Residual Income = ₹4,80,000

ATX (Advanced Taxation) — Strategic Professional

Double tax relief for cross-border income.

Part A — Multiple Choice Questions

QuestionOptionsAnswer & Why
The two principal methods of Double Tax Relief are:A) Deferral and Deduction
B) Exemption Method and Credit Method ✅
C) Amnesty and Penalty Waiver
D) Withholding and Refund		B — Under the Exemption Method, foreign income is simply excluded from home-country tax; under the Credit Method, foreign tax paid is credited against the home-country tax liability.

Part B — Scenario Question

📊 Double Tax Relief — Credit Method

Question: An Indian company earns foreign income of ₹10,00,000, on which foreign tax of ₹2,50,000 (25%) has already been paid. India's tax rate on this income is 30%. Under the ordinary credit method, calculate the net additional tax payable in India.

Step 1 — Indian tax on this income: 30% × ₹10,00,000 = ₹3,00,000
Step 2 — Relief allowed: LOWER of foreign tax paid (₹2,50,000) and Indian tax on that income (₹3,00,000) = ₹2,50,000
Step 3 — Net tax payable in India: ₹3,00,000 − ₹2,50,000

Answer: Net Additional Tax Payable in India = ₹50,000
